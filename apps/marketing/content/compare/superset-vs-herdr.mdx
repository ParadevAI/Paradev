Paradev and Herdr start from the same idea: coding agents should live in persistent terminals that outlive the window, grouped by the work they belong to. Since October 2026 both are also desktop apps, so the old "GUI or terminal" question no longer separates them.

What separates them now is how far each one goes past the terminal. Herdr goes wide on the machine in front of you: spaces, tabs, panes, mods you write in JavaScript, and any machine you can reach over SSH, on macOS, Linux, Windows, and phones. Paradev goes deep into the rest of the loop: diff review and pull requests in the app, agents started by schedules and events, and cloud workspaces your teammates can open and drive.

**Choose Herdr if you want a free, open-source, deeply customizable app over machines you own. Choose Paradev if you want review, automation, and a shared cloud workspace around the same agents.**

This comparison is published by Paradev. Herdr details were checked against [herdr.dev](https://herdr.dev), its [documentation](https://herdr.dev/docs/), its [gallery](https://herdr.dev/gallery), and the [herdrdev/herdr-app](https://github.com/herdrdev/herdr-app) and [herdrdev/herdr](https://github.com/herdrdev/herdr) repositories on November 10, 2026.

## First, Which Herdr?

Herdr is two programs, and they do not share agents.

- **Herdr TUI** (`herdrdev/herdr`) is the original terminal app with its own server, started in March 2026, Apache-2.0, around 50,000 GitHub stars, latest release v0.9.5 on October 14, 2026. It keeps working.
- **The Herdr desktop app** (`herdrdev/herdr-app`) is a new, separate native program built on `herdr-core`, with its own repository created on October 16, 2026 and a changelog that starts at 0.1.4 on October 23. It reached 0.1.15 on November 10, 2026.

Herdr's own FAQ puts it plainly: "They don't share agents, so what runs in one doesn't show up in the other." This comparison is about the desktop app unless it says otherwise.

## What Is Herdr Now?

The site's line is "Herdr is an app for running coding agents that you shape by asking," next to the claim "No two Herdrs look alike." That is the product in one sentence: the window, the sidebar, the tabs, the keys, and the themes are plain files under `~/.config/herdr-app`, a layout arranges the window, and mods are small JavaScript programs that draw panels, menus, and dialogs. You ask your agent to change them. The [gallery](https://herdr.dev/gallery) is where the community trades those files: on November 10, 2026 it listed 312 layouts and 576 mods, installed by copying one line for your agent to apply, not from a store.

The parts that matter for agent work are solid. A space holds work on one machine, usually one repository, and worktrees group in the sidebar. Each agent reports whether it is working, blocked, done, or idle, and the agents sidebar collects them from every space, including workers another agent started and sub-agents nested under their parent. Agents run in `herdr-core`, a small server, so closing the window leaves them running. `hc machine add` brings in any machine you can reach over SSH, Tailscale included, and remote work keeps going when your local window closes.

Herdr is free and Apache-2.0, needs no account, and ships fast: twelve releases from 0.1.4 on October 23 to 0.1.15 on November 10, 2026. Usage telemetry is on by default and can be turned off in Settings → Privacy.

## What Is Paradev?

Paradev is a desktop workspace for the CLI agents you already pay for. Each local workspace gets its own Git worktree, branch, and persistent terminals, and the agent runs with its own login, settings, and MCP servers. Around it are a Changes pane with diff review, commit, push, and pull request, a Pull Requests view with CI checks and line comments, a file editor, and an in-app browser with Design Mode. For Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, and Pi, a [chat interface](https://docs.paradev.com/chat) shows the same session as a chat.

Agents run in a background terminal daemon, so quitting with ⌘Q or installing an update leaves them working, and they reattach with scrollback. Work runs on your Mac, on hosts you add through Remote Access, on your own Linux server with [Bring Your Own Cloud](https://docs.paradev.com/bring-your-own-cloud), or in [cloud workspaces](/cloud). Paradev is open source under the MIT License.

## Key Differences

### Cloud workspaces

Herdr runs agents on machines you own: this laptop, or a server you add over SSH. That is a complete answer as long as a machine of yours is awake.

Paradev adds one that is not yours to keep awake. A [cloud workspace](/cloud) runs on its own cloud sandbox on the Team and Ultra plans, started from an environment that holds the repositories, the variables, and the region. Close the laptop and the agent keeps working; reopen the workspace from the desktop app, an iPhone, or an iPad. The agent never receives your keys: it sees a placeholder, and Paradev adds your sign-in to outgoing requests. Herdr has no cloud offering today.

### Multiplayer and teams

Herdr has no accounts and no team tier, by design, and nothing in its documentation shares a running session between people: your terminals never pass through anyone's servers. The collaboration it does have is between your own agents, with `hc run --bg` and `hc send`, and between people only through the gallery, where you copy somebody's layout.

Paradev shares the work itself. A cloud workspace can be visible to anyone in your organization or only to people you invite, and full access lets a teammate open its terminals and prompt the agents that are running. From a shell, `superset workspaces share add --user ann@example.com` or `--team` does the same thing. [Automations](https://docs.paradev.com/automations) run agents on a schedule or on an event, and each run becomes a live workspace you or a teammate can open, review, and continue. [Pages](https://docs.paradev.com/pages) give the team somewhere to discuss a result: an agent publishes a report or a design, teammates pin comments, and a watching agent publishes a new version. Larger teams get SAML SSO, SCIM, and audit logs on the enterprise tier, and Paradev is SOC 2 Type II. See [multiplayer coding agents](/multiplayer-coding-agents) for how those approaches compare.

### Review and the rest of the loop

This is the oldest difference between the two and it survived Herdr's move to a desktop app. In Paradev, you read the patch, edit a file, open the running app in a browser pane, open the pull request, watch its checks, and send a line comment back to the agent that wrote the code, without leaving the workspace. In Herdr, review happens in whatever you already use, in a pane beside the agent.

### Customization: files you shape vs features that ship

Herdr's bet is that you should be able to rebuild the app by asking an agent to edit JSON and JavaScript, and the gallery shows people doing it. The cost is stated in Herdr's own docs: mods are trusted code, not sandboxed extensions, so a mod you paste can do what you can do.

Paradev's bet is that the common shape is worth shipping: the panes, the diff, the pull requests, and the automations are features with defaults, and extension happens through plugins that add MCP tools, repository config, and themes. Less to shape, less to maintain.

### Platforms

Herdr is ahead here. It runs on macOS with Apple silicon, Linux on x86_64 and arm64, and Windows on x86_64, with apps for iPhone, iPad, and Android. Paradev runs on macOS, has an experimental Linux build, no Windows build yet, and an iPhone and iPad app, with Android on a [waitlist](/mobile#android).

### Agents that drive the tool

Both let agents operate the app. Herdr exposes `herdr-app --skill` and commands such as `ls`, `status`, `commands`, and `shot`. Paradev exposes a CLI, an MCP server, and a TypeScript SDK that go further out of the window: an agent can create a workspace, launch another agent in it, read its terminal, send a follow-up, open a pull request, or arrange panes, which is what the [orchestration skill](https://docs.paradev.com/orchestration) is built on.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose Herdr if you:**

- Want a free, Apache-2.0 app with no account, on macOS, Linux, or Windows
- Want to reshape the window, sidebar, keys, and panels by asking an agent to edit files
- Work across machines you own and reach over SSH or Tailscale
- Want phone apps on both iOS and Android

**Choose Paradev if you:**

- Want diff review, pull requests, CI checks, and line comments in the same app as the agent
- Want cloud workspaces that keep working when your laptop is closed
- Want a teammate to open the running workspace and prompt the agent
- Want agents started by schedules and by GitHub, Slack, or Linear events
- Want SSO, SCIM, and audit logs behind the tool

**Verdict:** Herdr is the better answer if the machine in front of you is the whole problem and you enjoy shaping your tools. Paradev is the better answer when the work has to outlive your laptop, be picked up by somebody else, or arrive reviewed. They are close enough in the terminal that the decision usually comes down to cloud, team, and review, not to panes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Herdr have a desktop app now?

Yes. `herdrdev/herdr-app` is a separate native app, Apache-2.0, released from October 2026 and at 0.1.15 on November 10, 2026. It is built on `herdr-core` and does not share agents with Herdr TUI, which keeps working.

### Is Herdr a Paradev alternative?

For organizing agent terminals on machines you own, yes. Both keep agents running outside the window, group work per repository, and let agents drive the app. Paradev adds diff review and pull requests, automations, cloud workspaces, and sharing with teammates.

### Can two people share a Herdr session?

No. Herdr has no accounts and no sharing; what it shares between people are layouts and mods in its gallery. In Paradev, a cloud workspace can be shared with your organization or named teammates, and full access lets them open its terminals and prompt the agents.

### Which runs on Windows?

Herdr does, on x86_64. Paradev runs on macOS, with an experimental Linux build and no Windows build yet.

### Are both open source?

Yes. Herdr's app, `herdr-core`, and the TUI are Apache-2.0. Paradev is MIT.
