1. Remove swap Shipped 18 November

PR: #9924, merged. 5 files, +86 −87. The production release that carries it succeeded, and boxes pick it up on their next wake.

What it does

Cloud boxes stop using swap. Removing swap alone was not safe: host-service stalled for up to 175 seconds. So the memory guard that kills runaway processes now checks four times as often and acts one step ahead.

Pseudocode

every 0.25 s:                              # was every 1 s
    read available memory                  # no process spawned
    drop = how much it fell since the last check
    if available - drop < 8% of the box:
        kill the largest ordinary process  # agents only if no ordinary process is left;
                                           # host-service and terminals never
        wait until it has really exited    # was a fixed 3 s pause
        check again at once

What a customer sees

  • A job bigger than the box is killed early. With swap it often finished slowly. A parallel build on a 4 GB box loses 3 of its 4 workers. A monorepo typecheck of about 8 GB is killed on the 4 GB size.
  • Within about 5 seconds the desktop shows an out-of-memory banner that names the process and offers a resize to the next size. The terminal shows Killed.
  • Host-service and terminals are never the ones killed. An agent is killed only when no ordinary process is left to kill. In every measured run the victim was an ordinary process.

Evidence

Measured on a real 2 vCPU / 4 GB Vercel box with its real memory limit, no swap, memguard from this PR:

CaseLongest host-service gapReapedKernel killsProtected processes killed
One steady allocator256 ms1, at 22.5 s0none
Parallel build, 4 workers256 ms3 of 4, first at 5.3 s0none
Page cache plus allocator256 ms1, at 28.8 s0none
Fast allocator, three runs254 ms each1 each, at 0.9 s0none

The worker's simulated runs at 4 GB and 8 GB gave the same picture, with a longest gap of 314 ms. On main, with swap and the 1-second check, the fast allocator caused stalls of 6 to 94 seconds in 6 of 7 simulated runs.

Not measured: snapshot size before and after on real Vercel. Swap stayed empty in every run, so the saving follows, but it has not been weighed.

Risk and rollback

  • The main risk is the product trade above: oversized jobs die where they used to finish.
  • Rollback is a revert. No boot step re-runs. Snapshots that already hold swap keep it either way.

Decision

Ship no swap with this loop? Decided: yes. Merged and released on 18 November.

Not blocking, worth scheduling: a killed Node process shows in the banner as "MainThread", and on the largest size the banner offers no next step.

2. Agents only from the drive A: shipped B: needs your decision

Update, 18 November: #9929 shipped, and its first release published all 19 regions in 113 seconds. #9943 follows up by keeping one previous bundle's drives, not two. #9911 is unblocked on sequencing; it needs decision 1 below and a second review.

PRs: #9929 (release side) and #9911 (box side, stacked on it). Both drafts.

What it does

Claude, Codex and Pdev exist twice today: on every box's disk and on the shared read-only agents drive. This removes the disk copy. It is split in two so nothing depends on deploy timing.

  • A, #9929 changes only the release. It changes nothing on any box and can soak alone.
  • B, #9911 removes the disk copy and the flag. It merges only after a release with A has succeeded.

Pseudocode

# A: the release
publish the agents drive in all 19 regions
fail the release if a region with an environment has no drive,
    or a drive without claude, codex or pdev
move every environment row to the new bundle, archived ones too
keep the drives of the 3 newest bundles          # so a rollback has something to mount
only the production release prunes drives

# B: create or wake a box
always try to mount the region's agents drive    # no flag
retry on a transient error
still no drive -> open the box anyway, report it to us
boot:
    link agents from the drive
    only after a successful link: remove the copies on the box's disk
agent launched with no drive:
    "Agents are unavailable on this box; reopen the workspace"

What a customer sees

  • When it works: nothing. Same agents, same paths.
  • When the drive cannot be mounted: the box opens with terminals, files and desktop working, and launching an agent gives the message above.
  • Workspaces of an archived environment move to the current bundle on their next wake. Three such workspaces exist in production.

Evidence

  • Real Vercel, on an earlier head of B: a drive was published for the branch's bundle, all three agents ran from it, and they were linked again after a stop and wake.
  • Local image: 4.89 GB to 4.10 GB.
  • The agents drive has been on for every organization since 17 November, 23:38 UTC. Two customer organizations were seen picking it up, one on create and one on wake.

Not verified: the current heads of A and B on real Vercel (A's run is in progress), how long publishing 19 regions takes, and a rollback rehearsal.

Risk and rollback

  • An independent review of the first version said "do not merge". It found that rollback did not restore agents, that an incomplete drive could ship, and that archived environments' workspaces could never wake. A and B are the response. They have not been re-reviewed.
  • A release can now fail on a drive problem. Boxes then keep the previous bundle for one more cycle.
  • Rollback of B: revert it. Boxes that already dropped their disk copy get it back, because the install marker is now removed with it. The kept drives cover the previous bundles.
  • B conflicts with the workspace drive (#9847) in six files. Whichever lands second rebases.

Your decisions

No drive: open without agents, or fail the open?

Recommendation: open without agents, which is what B implements. A box with working terminals and files is better than no box, and it means a drive problem can never mark a workspace failed.

Should workspaces of archived environments move to the current bundle?

Recommendation: yes. Otherwise their drive is pruned and they lose agents.

Drives in all 19 regions on every release, or only regions with an environment?

Recommendation: all 19, since an environment can be created in any region before the next release. The cost is a longer release and more ways for the nightly to fail. The first release on A will give the real publish time.

3. Environment builds on Vercel Workflow Decided; ready for your review

PR: #9906, draft, stacked on your #9282.

What it does

A build is one serverless function today, with a 285-second budget. This makes it a Vercel Workflow run: a series of short steps, each retried on failure, with no compute used while it waits.

Pseudocode

environmentBuildWorkflow(buildId):
    deadline = now + 10 min
    create the build box                   # found by name on retry, never a second box
    for command in [checkout, install]:
        start it detached on the box       # runs once per command
        until it exits:
            save the last 40 lines to the build row
            sleep 5 s
    stop the box, poll until the snapshot exists
    activate the new golden                # no second swap on retry
    on any failure: delete the box, mark the build failed with the reason

What a customer sees

  • A build can take up to 10 minutes. Today an install over about 3 minutes fails. A longer build stops with "The build ran for more than 10 minutes and was stopped".
  • Same build IDs, statuses and table columns. No tRPC output changes.
  • The stored log updates every 5 seconds during a build. Nothing in the UI reads it yet.

Evidence

  • A local run of the API app with Workflow's local runner and a fake build box: a successful build and a cancelled build both ran end to end.
  • Tests with no real waiting: slow install, failed install, a step that fails then succeeds on retry, the limit, cleanup.
  • Preview deploys of API, Web, Docs and Marketing pass on the newer CLI.
  • Cost at Vercel's listed price: about $0.004 for a typical build, $0.012 for a 10-minute one, in Workflow events. The build box itself (8 vCPU, 16 GB) costs at most about $0.23 for 10 minutes.

Not verified: a real build on Vercel's workflow runtime with a real sandbox. A run stays pinned to the deployment that started it; one whose deployment is deleted is failed by the stale check after 20 minutes.

Decided

  • Vercel CLI 50.31.0 for every deploy: shipped in #9980; the production deploy passed on it.
  • Build limit: 10 minutes, a hard ceiling. Environments will rebuild daily, so a longer limit does not scale in cost.
  • #9282 is rebased onto main (migration 0199); #9906 sits on it (migration 0200). Preview deploys pass on both.

Also needed before it runs anywhere: "Enable access to System Environment Variables" on the API's Vercel project. Nobody has checked whether it is on.

4. Workspace drive Ready for review

PRs: #9915 (the migration, lands first), #9847 (the change, at 8459d3c3), #9914 (the TLA+ model). All drafts, CI green.

What it does

A workspace's work moves onto a Vercel Drive mounted beside the box, so the box's own snapshot stays small however much work is done. Everything still persists. It is behind a per-environment switch, off everywhere.

Pseudocode

create a workspace, drive mode on:
    fork the environment's golden drive        # about 1 s, copy-on-write
    mount it; first boot lays it out and records the owner on the drive
boot:
    drive expected but not mounted, or not this workspace's -> refuse to boot
    a bundle without drive support -> refuse before switching to it
    bind /workspace, home caches and docker data onto the drive
    fresh fork -> fetch and create the branch, 3 tries;
                  if all fail, keep the golden copy and refetch next boot
    existing work -> never replaced
wake:
    under one lock keyed on the drive: wait for it to be released, mount, resume
create job:
    may fail or stop a workspace only while it is still being created,
    or after this same job brought it up
    a lock timeout is a retry, never a failure
delete:
    retire the box and drive names in the same write
    cleanup after the grace period, never of anything a live row names
undo delete:
    resumes the same box and drive if they still exist

What a customer sees

  • Nothing changes while the switch is off for their environment.
  • With it on: the same workspace, a smaller snapshot, and everything kept across stop and wake.
  • bun install is 5 to 10% slower on the drive; create is about 1 second slower for the fork.

The rules, and the result

The model's author derived it from the code at 8459d3c3, independently of the fix's author. Every fix it relies on was found as a real guard at a file and line. All ten rules hold, across seven scenarios and about 6.9 million states, and at deeper bounds for the riskiest scenarios. Turning off any one guard brings its bug back.

RuleHarm if broken
Nothing is lostCommits or changes disappear
Never empty shown as workUser sees an empty workspace as their project
One writer per driveDrive reads as corrupt or empty
Cleanup never deletes a live workspaceA workspace in use loses its files
Only creating a workspace gives it a driveOld workspaces break when drive mode turns on
No drive, no changeThe switch being off is not safe
Never stuck for everWork is safe but cannot be opened
A new workspace starts on fresh codeStale code that looks like the user's branch
A passing glitch is not a failureA workspace that would come up shows as broken
No drive left behind for everStorage billed for ever (holds only while the sweep is run)

What the guarantee does not cover:

  • More than one workspace and one environment, or combinations across scenarios.
  • Platform behaviour nobody has measured, such as what Vercel does with a mount that names a deleted drive.
  • A lock held for more than about 6 minutes, which still fails a workspace that is being created.
  • A gitignored file at a path the base branch tracks, which a fresh workspace's first checkout could overwrite.
  • Agent drives, home caches, Docker, regions, promoting the source workspace itself, and boxes leaked by a failed create (storage only).

It took six rounds to get here. In three of them a fix introduced a new lifecycle bug that only the model caught, and once the fix's author edited the model in a way that hid two bugs. The model's skill now forbids a product PR from editing the model.

Evidence on real Vercel

Same work on both boxes (clone, bun install, 4 GB written). Ranges across nine runs:

Plain boxDrive box
Root snapshot after stop7.86 to 8.02 GB2.58 to 2.79 GB
Create to host-service ready4.7 to 5.3 s3.7 to 6.4 s
Wake, total5.6 to 9.4 s5.2 to 6.8 s
  • Kept across stop and wake: an uncommitted file, a local commit, a home file, a root-disk file, a Docker image.
  • After an unclean stop: the box boots, host.db passes its integrity check, the checkout is intact.
  • A workspace created from a drive-mode golden boots on its new branch with the golden's dependencies.
  • That last check failed once in six runs at an earlier commit: a failed first fetch made the box re-clone and lose the dependencies. A fresh workspace now never re-clones, and the check prints the workspace's own logs if it fails again. It has passed every run since.

Not measured: a cold wake, where the smaller snapshot should pay off most and which cannot be forced from the SDK. The wake numbers were probably all warm, so do not read a wake improvement from them.

Risk and rollback

  • The switch is per environment. Turning it off stops new drives; workspaces that already have a drive keep it.
  • Rollback of the code is a revert after the switch is off everywhere. The migration only adds nullable columns.
  • Still unbuilt: moving existing workspaces onto drives, and scheduling the orphan sweep, which is manual and dry-run.
  • #9911 (agents, box side) and this PR conflict in six files. Whichever lands second rebases.

Your decisions

Enable it for one internal environment, new workspaces only?

Recommendation: yes. The model and the real runs agree, the switch is per environment, and existing workspaces are untouched.

Which environment?

Recommendation: the Paradev environment we use ourselves, since that is where we would notice a problem first.

What happens to a drive when its workspace's snapshot expires after 30 days?

Today the snapshot expires and the drive does not, so an abandoned workspace keeps billing for a drive nothing can resume.

Recommendation: delete the drive with the snapshot, through the same retried cleanup. Not needed before an internal trial; needed before customers.
