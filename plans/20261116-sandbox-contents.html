Why this matters. Vercel restores a stopped sandbox at about 1 GB/s from a snapshot that, for us, is almost never cached: 1.7k creations against 800 unique snapshots, most used once. Every gigabyte in a workspace's snapshot is roughly one second on every cold wake. The swap alone is eight of them.

Reading it

What the inventory says, in order of weight.

  1. Swap is a third of the disk and all of it is waste at stop time. It exists because the dev stack needs more than 8 GB on a 4 vCPU box. It is written to the snapshot because nothing releases it before the platform's idle stop.
  2. Dependencies are the biggest legitimate item, and they are two copies that only look like one because of hardlinks. A drive holding the repo must hold the bun cache too, or bun falls back to copying.
  3. The image is 5.4 GB and about 2.5 GB of it is speculative: three LLVMs, a JDK, Go, Docker, emacs, ffmpeg and ImageMagick. Whether that costs anything per wake depends on whether a fork's snapshot is a delta over the golden, which one call to Snapshot.list (it returns sizeBytes and parentId) will settle.
  4. 1.5 GB is certain, cheap cleanup: the npm cache the golden hook leaves in /root/.npm, the staged archives kept after their install step, and the Electron download caches.
  5. Build output and profiles are small today but grow with every session, and they are the part that rewrites on every stop.

Method: du -x over the root filesystem from inside the sandbox, which counts hardlinked files once per invocation; df reports 25 GB used while the layers sum to about 23 GB, the rest being XFS metadata and log. Apt groups are pattern matches over dpkg-query installed sizes, so a package that fits two groups is counted in both. Running processes from ps and ss at the time of measurement. The boot-time context is from Vercel's Sandbox engineer in #ext-vercel, 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-06, and plans/20260913-sandbox-start-time.md.
